## 📊 Dashboard Insights

### Page 1: Executive Overview

**What it shows:**
An overview of the attrition problem, highlighting its overall size and where it is concentrated through headline KPIs, attrition by job role, department, and overtime status.

#### Key Findings

* Overall attrition is **16.1%** (**237 of 1,470 employees**).
* **Sales Representatives** have the highest attrition rate at **39.8%**, compared with **2.5%** for Research Directors.
* **Sales (20.6%)** and **Human Resources (19.0%)** have higher attrition rates than **R&D (13.8%)**, although R&D has the highest number of leavers by headcount.
* Employees working **overtime** have a **30.5%** attrition rate, compared with **10.4%** for employees who do not work overtime.

**Recommendation:**
Prioritize retention efforts for **Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians**, as these roles show disproportionately high attrition.

---

### Page 2: Compensation & Tenure

**What it shows:**
An analysis of whether attrition is associated with compensation, employee tenure, and previous work experience, using income-vs-tenure analysis, income gaps by role, and attrition by number of previous employers.

#### Key Findings

* Leavers earn an average of **$4,787/month**, compared with **$6,833/month** for employees who stay, representing a **$2,046 income gap**.
* Leavers have shorter tenure, with an average of **5.1 years** at exit compared with **7.4 years** for retained employees.
* Attrition by number of previous employers follows a **U-shaped pattern**:

  * **0–1:** 16.9%
  * **2–3:** 10.5%
  * **4–5:** 16.3%
  * **6+:** 20.8%
* The largest income gaps appear in **Human Resources, Research Scientist, Sales Representative, and Laboratory Technician** roles.
* Some senior roles show the opposite pattern, but these results should be interpreted cautiously because of smaller sample sizes.

**Recommendation:**
Benchmark compensation for roles with the widest income gaps and strengthen **onboarding and early-tenure check-ins**, particularly during the first **1–2 years**.

---

### Page 3: Risk Segments

**What it shows:**
A deeper look at employee segments with higher observed attrition using a **Job Level × Overtime** matrix, a headline risk indicator, and a ranked segment table. Segments with fewer than 20 employees are excluded.

#### Key Findings

* Employees who are **Job Level 1 + Overtime + Single** have a **67.8% attrition rate (n=59)** — more than four times the overall company rate.
* Attrition remains high across all marital-status groups within **Level 1 + Overtime**:

  * Divorced: **44.1%**
  * Married: **42.9%**
  * Single: **67.8%**
* The results indicate that **entry-level employees working overtime** are a particularly high-risk segment, while marital status appears to further differentiate the observed risk.

**Recommendation:**
Review and rebalance overtime among **Level 1 employees**, starting with the highest-risk segment. Consider pairing workload changes with clearer **promotion and pay-progression paths** for entry-level employees.

---

## 🎯 Overall Recommendations

1. **Reduce entry-level overtime** and monitor attrition among Level 1 employees.
2. **Target Sales Representatives** with compensation and workload reviews.
3. **Investigate compensation gaps** in roles where leavers earn noticeably less than retained employees.
4. **Strengthen early-tenure support** for new hires, particularly during the first 1–2 years.
5. **Track risk-segment KPIs**, such as Level 1 + Overtime attrition, to evaluate whether retention initiatives are associated with improvement.

> **⚠️ Important Note:** This analysis is based on a **single snapshot with no time dimension**. The findings therefore show **associations, not proven causal relationships**. While the relationship between overtime and attrition is strong in this dataset, a controlled pilot or longitudinal analysis would be needed to assess whether reducing overtime actually reduces attrition.
