Page 1: Executive Overview

What it shows: The size of the attrition problem and where it concentrates, using headline KPIs, attrition rate by job role, and attrition by department.

Key findings

Overall attrition is 16.1% (237 of 1,470 employees).
Sales Representatives leave at 39.8%, against 2.5% for Research Directors.
Sales (20.6%) and HR (19.0%) run hotter than R&D (13.8%), even though R&D has the most leavers by headcount.
Employees working overtime leave at 30.5%, versus 10.4% for those who don't.

Recommendation: Prioritize retention effort on Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians first, since those roles drive disproportionate losses.

Page 2: Compensation & Tenure

What it shows: Whether attrition is about pay, experience, or both, using the income-vs-tenure scatter, income gap by role, and attrition by prior companies worked.

Key findings

Leavers earned $4,787 a month on average versus $6,833 for stayers, a $2,046 gap.
Leavers go earlier: 5.1 years average tenure at exit versus 7.4 for those who stay.
Attrition by prior employers is U-shaped: 16.9% (0-1), 10.5% (2-3), 16.3% (4-5), 20.8% (6+). The newest and the most mobile employees both churn more.
The pay gap is largest in HR, Research Scientist, Sales Rep, and Lab Tech roles. For a few senior roles it reverses on small samples, so treat those cautiously.

Recommendation: Benchmark pay for the roles with the widest gaps, and invest in onboarding and early-tenure check-ins (first 1-2 years), when risk is highest.

Page 3: Risk Segments

What it shows: Exactly who is most likely to leave, using the job level x overtime matrix, a headline callout, and a ranked segment table (segments under 20 employees excluded).

Key findings

Job Level 1 + overtime + single: 67.8% attrition (n=59), more than 4x the company average.
Every marital status within Level 1 + overtime sits above 42% (divorced 44.1%, married 42.9%).
That makes entry-level overtime the driver; marital status only amplifies it.

Recommendation: Cap or rebalance overtime for Level 1 staff, and pilot this with the highest-risk group first. Pair it with a promotion or pay-progression path for entry-level roles.

Overall recommendation summary
Reduce entry-level overtime, the biggest and most actionable lever.
Target Sales Representatives with pay review and workload changes.
Close pay gaps in the roles where leavers earn noticeably less.
Strengthen early-tenure support for new hires.
Track a risk-segment KPI (e.g. Level 1 + overtime attrition) so you can measure whether interventions work.

One caveat to state openly if asked: this is a single snapshot with no dates, so the findings show association, not proven cause. The overtime link is strong, but a pilot would be the way to confirm it.
